P/E Ratio Medium · 2/5
The Price-to-Earnings ratio measures how much investors pay per dollar of earnings. Formula: Stock Price ÷ EPS. A high P/E may reflect growth expectations; a low P/E may signal undervaluation — or poor prospects.
Source: Investopedia — Price-to-Earnings Ratio
🤖 AI Explanation Think of it like buying a café. If it earns $10k/year and costs $150k, your P/E is 15 — you're paying 15 years of earnings upfront. A P/E of 30 means you're betting on strong future growth.
A stock trades at $100 with EPS of $5. What is its P/E ratio?

Volatility Medium · 2/5
Volatility measures how much a security's price fluctuates over time. A highly volatile asset swings widely; a low-volatility asset moves gradually. Measured by standard deviation or beta.
Source: Investopedia — Volatility
🤖 AI Explanation Imagine two rollercoasters — one with gentle hills, one with steep drops. Both go from A to B, but the stomach-churning one is more "volatile." Higher volatility = higher risk AND higher potential reward.
A stock that swings 20% in a week vs one that moves 1% — which is more volatile?
